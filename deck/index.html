The whole thing in 30 seconds

Marcus has a powerful gaming PC that sits idle while he's at work. He joins AI for All and decides to earn from 70% of his available capacity and donate 30%.

A company that needs AI infrastructure pays us to process its workloads. We route an appropriate workload to Marcus's computer, his local AI processes it, Marcus gets paid, and we take a small platform fee.

Then Maya, a student who can't afford AI, needs tutoring. She pays nothing. Her request uses the capacity Marcus chose to donate — or capacity funded by a corporate sponsor. Marcus never sees her conversation; he just sees that his computer helped give another person access to AI.

So businesses fund the marketplace, contributors provide the infrastructure, and donated or sponsored capacity expands access to people who otherwise wouldn't have it.
